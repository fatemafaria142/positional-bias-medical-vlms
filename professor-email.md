My core research question is: When a model is given a text prompt that specifies a spatial position, such as “left” or “right,” for an anatomical feature in a bilateral medical image, does it actually segment or localize the correct anatomical structure based on the image, or does it rely on memorized anatomical knowledge or other shortcuts?
To answer this question, I have conducted experiments across 3 different phases: 
2D zero-shot grounding benchmark: Tested 11 VLMs (PaliGemma 2, Qwen2.5-VL, LocateAnything-3B, etc.) on 2D medical images with left/right/upper/lower prompts to see if they respond to positional words at all and found that most models ignored the positional word entirely (near chance-level or no output shift).
Text-Guided 3D segmentation model benchmark: Evaluated 3 candidate 3D text-guided segmentation models (Text3DSAM, Medical-SAM3, and VoxTell) with left/right positional prompts and found that VoxTell was accurate enough to segment both smaller and large organs, while the other two did not produce usable segmentations. 
Rotation Probes for Positional Grounding: Applied true anatomical rotations at 30°, 60°, 90°, 120°, 150°, and 180° to test spatial orientation.
